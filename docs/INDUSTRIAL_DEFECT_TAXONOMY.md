## 1. Executive Summary & Purpose

Videoscope inspection of internal turbine, engine, gearbox, and piping components introduces unique optical challenges: monocular perspective, intense direct coaxial LED illumination, specular highlights, steep angle distortion, and deep shadows.

Historically, automated vision systems fail in videoscope environments by conflating **superficial visual features** with **critical structural failure modes**. For example:
- A deliberate through-wall dilution hole or cooling aperture is misclassified as a "crack" or "void" because both present dark, high-contrast interior regions.
- A shallow surface scratch is misclassified as a structural fatigue crack due to linear aspect ratio, triggering unwarranted engine teardowns.
- Surface iron oxide staining (rust) is misclassified as structural cavitation or base material thinning.

This document establishes a **rigorous 15-class industrial defect taxonomy** tailored to internal videoscope non-destructive testing (NDT). It explicitly decouples **geometric visual morphology** from **metallurgical / engineering diagnosis**, defines strict **inclusion/exclusion boundaries**, and introduces **ambiguity resolution rules**.

---

## 2. Core Methodological Principles

### 2.1 Separation of Geometric Appearance vs. Engineering Diagnosis
A 2D monocular videoscope image records reflected photons, not stress tensors, material thickness, or ultrasonic time-of-flight.
- **Visual Morphology**: What is directly observable (e.g., "linear dark discontinuity with irregular branching edges", "elliptical sharp-edged void with unreflective interior").
- **Engineering Diagnosis**: The underlying metallurgical mechanism (e.g., "high-cycle fatigue crack", "casting shrinkage cavity", "spark-machined cooling hole").
- **Rule**: Bounding box and segmentation labels must be grounded in observable visual morphology. Where engineering intent is known from CAD/schematics (e.g., design feature vs. defect), that knowledge must be captured in metadata, not by hallucinating depth.

### 2.2 Depth and Through-Wall Penetration Disclaimer
**An image alone cannot prove wall thickness, depth of penetration, or subsurface propagation.** Without stereo-measurement probes, phase-shift optical 3D scanning, or eddy-current verification:
- Deep pits and through-holes can appear identical under front-facing illumination.
- Surface scratches cannot be certified as zero-depth without cross-lighting or tactile probe assessment.
- Any finding classified as high severity based solely on visual contrast is provisional until validated by an inspector.

### 2.3 Ground Truth Integrity
- No label may be generated by unsupervised heuristic or unverified model output.
- All labels in training and evaluation datasets must originate from human inspector verification.

### Class 1: Crack
* **Definition**: A planar mechanical, fatigue, or stress discontinuity exhibiting a jagged, non-uniform linear trajectory with sharp propagating tips.
* **Visual Morphology**: Narrow, tortuous or meandering line; sharp aspect ratio (length >> width); variable aperture width; shadowing along one edge depending on illumination angle; lack of specular reflection from the crack trough.
* **Inclusion Criteria**:
  - Linear or branched fissure with irregular, jagged trajectory.
  - Tapered or razor-sharp terminations.
  - Follows stress concentrations, fillets, welds, or blade root edges.
* **Exclusion Criteria**:
  - Perfectly straight, uniform-width lines with smooth parallel borders -> Class 5 (Scratch) or machining mark.
  - Network of uniform, hexagonal/crazed micro-lines across hot-gas components -> Class 13 (Thermal Cracking).
  - Circular or rounded voids -> Class 2 (Hole), Class 3 (Pitting), or Class 4 (Cavity).
* **Ambiguity Rule**: If illumination directly over-saturates the trough making depth impossible to determine, and path is straight, classify as Class 15 (Unknown) or Class 5 (Scratch) with `inspector_flag=NEEDS_STEREO_MEASUREMENT`.

---

### Class 2: Hole
* **Definition**: A discrete through-aperture or deep geometric opening, either intentional (engineered cooling hole, oil passage, drainage port) or catastrophic (puncture, burn-through breach).
* **Visual Morphology**: Regular geometric perimeter (circular, oval, or laser-drilled oblong); distinct chamfer or sharp boundary edge; interior completely dark or opening to internal cavity/chamber without visible floor.
* **Inclusion Criteria**:
  - Clear closed boundary with interior darker than surrounding surface.
  - Engineered aperture matching blueprint locations (cooling holes on turbine blades/vanes).
  - Foreign object impact punch-through.
* **Exclusion Criteria**:
  - Shallow depression with visible rough floor -> Class 3 (Pitting) or Class 8 (Dent).
  - Narrow elongated fissure -> Class 1 (Crack).
  - Irregular casting void formed during solidification -> Class 4 (Cavity / Void).
  - Gas pocket in weld bead -> Class 12 (Weld Porosity).
* **Ambiguity Rule**: Engineered holes are **non-defective design features** unless blocked or cracked at rim. In the detector taxonomy, mark `is_design_feature=True` in metadata. If an engineered hole exhibits radiating cracks, annotate the hole as `Hole` and the radiating lines as `Crack`.

---

### Class 3: Pitting
* **Definition**: Highly localized, deep, cavity-like material loss typically caused by chemical/electrochemical attack, cavitation, or cyclic rolling contact micro-fatigue.
* **Visual Morphology**: Small clusters or isolated round-to-irregular craters; dark shadowed centers with bright specular halo rims caused by steep crater lip edges; rough, uneven interior floor.
* **Inclusion Criteria**:
  - Clustered or isolated depressions with high diameter-to-depth ratio (pit diameter typically < 3 mm).
  - Rough, etched, or stepped bottom surface visible under angled lighting.
* **Exclusion Criteria**:
  - Smooth depression without rim lip or material removal -> Class 8 (Dent).
  - Broad, diffuse surface oxidation without distinct individual crater walls -> Class 7 (Corrosion) or Class 6 (Rust).
  - Through-wall complete penetration -> Class 2 (Hole).
* **Ambiguity Rule**: Distinguish from weld porosity by location: pitting occurs across parent material subjected to corrosive/abrasive fluids or rolling surfaces; porosity occurs exclusively on or immediately adjacent to weld beads.

---

### Class 4: Cavity / Void
* **Definition**: A macroscopic three-dimensional hollow, pocket, or material deficit in base metal, casting, or lining that does not penetrate through the opposing wall.
* **Visual Morphology**: Irregular, rounded or amoeboid 3D depression; shadowed interior recesses; distinct depth variation across the feature floor.
* **Inclusion Criteria**:
  - Substantial non-through void (larger than pitting, non-planar unlike cracks).
  - Casting shrinkage voids, internal blowout pockets, or deep erosion pockets.
* **Exclusion Criteria**:
  - Engineered through-hole -> Class 2 (Hole).
  - Localized micro-craters < 2 mm -> Class 3 (Pitting).
  - Flat surface spall where a thin layer detached -> Class 9 (Chipping / Spalling).

---

### Class 5: Scratch
* **Definition**: A shallow, mechanical abrasive groove or mark caused by sliding contact with foreign grit, tools, or handling equipment.
* **Visual Morphology**: Continuous, straight or sweeping arc; uniform width; visible parallel longitudinal micro-striations; raised burr or specular reflection along one or both flanks; no dark deep shadow abyss.
* **Inclusion Criteria**:
  - Straight or sweeping line with uniform trajectory and constant width.
  - Bright reflective ridges along edges caused by plastic metal displacement (plowing).
  - Base of the groove remains visibly illuminated and reflective.
* **Exclusion Criteria**:
  - Jagged, meandering path with sharp propagating tip -> Class 1 (Crack).
  - Broad shallow depression without groove walls -> Class 8 (Dent).
  - Deep groove exhibiting sub-surface shadowing or branching -> Class 1 (Crack).
* **Ambiguity Rule**: If a line originates at a high-stress radius (e.g. blade fillet) and shows any zig-zag trajectory, default to Class 1 (Crack) pending magnetic particle or eddy current verification.

---

### Class 6: Rust
* **Definition**: Specific oxidation of iron/steel forming reddish-brown, orange, or yellowish iron oxide flakes or powdery patina on the surface.
* **Visual Morphology**: Characteristic chromatic signature (amber, orange, reddish-brown, ochre); dusty, granular, or matte texture; often follows liquid pooling streaks or condensation paths.
* **Inclusion Criteria**:
  - Chromatic ferrous oxidation without measurable metal displacement or deep cratering.
  - Surface dust or discoloration that can be wiped clean during service.
* **Exclusion Criteria**:
  - Base metal pitting, etching, or material thickness loss -> Class 7 (Corrosion) or Class 3 (Pitting).
  - Dark grey/green/white non-ferrous oxides (e.g. aluminum, titanium, nickel superalloys) -> Class 7 (Corrosion).
* **Ambiguity Rule**: Rust is purely a superficial chemical patina. When rust has initiated cratering, annotate the craters as Class 3 (Pitting) and the surrounding discolored field as Class 6 (Rust) or Class 7 (Corrosion).

---

### Class 7: Corrosion
* **Definition**: General chemical or electrochemical degradation and consumption of the metallic substrate, including uniform thinning, galvanic attack, intergranular attack, and non-ferrous oxidation (e.g. white rust on zinc/aluminum, green patina on copper, oxide scale on superalloys).
* **Visual Morphology**: Diffuse, mottled surface roughness; loss of original specular machining reflectivity; mottled discoloration (white, green, black, dull grey); flaky or blistered surface topography.
* **Inclusion Criteria**:
  - General surface breakdown, etching, scaling, or blistering.
  - Chemical degradation across non-ferrous metals or complex superalloys.
* **Exclusion Criteria**:
  - Distinct individual pits with steep rim walls -> Class 3 (Pitting).
  - Simple loose orange iron oxide film -> Class 6 (Rust).
  - External dirt or soot without substrate attack -> Class 14 (Dirt / Deposit).

---

### Class 8: Dent
* **Definition**: A localized plastic deformation or depression caused by mechanical impact from a blunt object, without through-wall rupture or loss of metal volume.
* **Visual Morphology**: Smooth, rounded concave depression; continuous surface texture without tearing or fractured edges; smooth shadow transition across the concave basin; displaced rim ridge (crowning) around the perimeter.
* **Inclusion Criteria**:
  - Concave deformation with intact, unbroken surface skin.
  - Smooth illumination gradient (gradual light-to-shadow falloff).
* **Exclusion Criteria**:
  - Surface with sharp missing fragments -> Class 9 (Chipping / Spalling).
  - Penetrating rupture -> Class 2 (Hole).
  - Sharp gouge with plowed metal burrs -> Class 5 (Scratch).

---

### Class 9: Chipping / Spalling
* **Definition**: Detachment or flaking of discrete surface flakes, coating layers (thermal barrier coatings, chrome plating), or sharp metal fragments due to shear fatigue or brittle fracture.
* **Visual Morphology**: Irregular jagged perimeter with steep step-down cliff; exposed under-layer or substrate with contrasting color or texture; flat-bottomed crater; sharp, crisp boundary fracture lines.
* **Inclusion Criteria**:
  - Missing coating layer exposing metallic substrate.
  - Fragmented, chipped edge on gear teeth, turbine blade leading/trailing edges, or valve seats.
* **Exclusion Criteria**:
  - Rounded depression from blunt force -> Class 8 (Dent).
  - Deep cratering from chemical attack -> Class 3 (Pitting).
  - Planar fissure without material detachment -> Class 1 (Crack).

---

### Class 10: Excess Weld Metal
* **Definition**: Weld reinforcement extending beyond the specified design profile, crown height, or root pass envelope (weld bead overfill).
* **Visual Morphology**: Bulbous, convex bead protruding above the adjacent base metal plane; visible solidification ripples (scallops); smooth transition or sharp re-entrant toe angle.
* **Inclusion Criteria**:
  - Bulging or excessively high weld crown or root bead.
  - Over-welded joint profile along designated weld seams.
* **Exclusion Criteria**:
  - Isolated detached globules -> Class 11 (Weld Spatter).
  - Internal hollow bubbles -> Class 12 (Weld Porosity).
  - Base metal deformation -> Class 8 (Dent).

---

### Class 11: Weld Spatter
* **Definition**: Discrete spherical or droplet globules of expelled molten filler metal deposited and bonded onto adjacent base metal or weld surfaces during arc welding.
* **Visual Morphology**: Small spherical or teardrop-shaped beads; raised convex profile; strong specular highlight at apex with crescent shadow at base; scattered distribution within 50 mm of weld seam.
* **Inclusion Criteria**:
  - Round globules adhered to surface in heat-affected zone.
* **Exclusion Criteria**:
  - Smooth continuous weld bead overfill -> Class 10 (Excess Weld Metal).
  - Dark concave pits -> Class 3 (Pitting) or Class 12 (Weld Porosity).
  - Loose foreign particulate -> Class 14 (Dirt / Deposit).

---

### Class 12: Weld Porosity
* **Definition**: Cavities or gas entrapment pockets formed in the weld pool during solidification, appearing at the weld face or revealed by post-weld machining.
* **Visual Morphology**: Spherical or tubular dark pinholes situated directly on the weld bead or in the heat-affected fusion zone; smooth interior contour; high contrast dark center under normal videoscope lighting.
* **Inclusion Criteria**:
  - Gas bubbles or clusters confined strictly to weld seam geometry.
* **Exclusion Criteria**:
  - Parent material localized erosion/corrosion -> Class 3 (Pitting).
  - General casting shrinkage pockets -> Class 4 (Cavity / Void).
  - Cracks at weld toe or crater -> Class 1 (Crack).

---

### Class 13: Thermal Cracking
* **Definition**: A network or mesh of shallow micro-fissures (thermal fatigue crazing / alligatoring) induced by severe cyclic thermal gradients on hot-section components (combustor liners, turbine vanes, brake disks).
* **Visual Morphology**: Interconnected polygonal, hexagonal, or mosaic mesh pattern resembling dried cracked mud; numerous intersecting nodes; shallow depth relative to primary structural cracks.
* **Inclusion Criteria**:
  - Multi-directional interconnected craze network on high-temperature alloy surfaces.
  - Associated with thermal discoloration (straw, blue, purple temper shades or oxidized scale).
* **Exclusion Criteria**:
  - Single dominant propagating fissure -> Class 1 (Crack).
  - Superficial mechanical scratches -> Class 5 (Scratch).

---

### Class 14: Dirt / Deposit
* **Definition**: Extraneous non-structural particulate, combustion soot, unburned fuel residue, baked lubricant sludge, scale, or foreign debris resting upon or adhering to the component surface.
* **Visual Morphology**: Amorphous or irregular mass; lacks geometric boundary; dull, non-metallic, or matte appearance; variable color (black soot, white salt deposits, yellow sulfur cake); irregular height profile without underlying substrate damage.
* **Inclusion Criteria**:
  - Surface accumulation without loss of base metal integrity.
  - Particulate heaps, greasy oil films, combustion carbon cake.
* **Exclusion Criteria**:
  - Adherent oxidized base metal degradation -> Class 6 (Rust) or Class 7 (Corrosion).
  - Missing material -> Class 9 (Chipping / Spalling).

---

### Class 15: Unknown / Ambiguous
* **Definition**: Any visual feature, shadow, reflection anomaly, lens flare, optical blur, or obscured region where unambiguous classification into Classes 1–14 cannot be established with confidence >= 0.70 by visual criteria alone.
* **Visual Morphology**: Ambiguous dark regions obscured by steep glare, out-of-focus optics, extreme distance from probe tip, or ambiguous geometric overlap.
* **Inclusion Criteria**:
  - Features where inspector cannot confidently distinguish scratch vs crack, or shadow vs pit.
  - Any artifact requiring supplemental NDT (eddy current, ultrasonic, penetrant) or probe repositioning.
* **Rule**: When in doubt, label as Class 15. **Never force an ambiguous finding into a critical structural class (like Crack) without evidence.**

## 4. Key Distinction Matrix

| Feature | Primary Distinguishing Visual Signature | Common Confusion & How to Resolve |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Hole vs. Crack** | Hole has circular/elliptical smooth closed perimeter with uniform dark core; crack is narrow, jagged, tortuous with propagating sharp tips. | Under direct coaxial light, both have dark cores. Check edge irregularity and aspect ratio: aspect ratio > 10:1 with zig-zag edge = Crack. |
| **Scratch vs. Crack** | Scratch has straight/arc path, constant width, reflective plowed ridges on flank, reflective bottom; crack has jagged trajectory, dark shadow trough, no plowed ridges. | If line is perfectly straight with shiny edges = Scratch. If line wanders across grain boundaries = Crack. |
| **Hole vs. Pitting** | Hole has smooth machined or punch-through edge and no visible bottom; pit has rough irregular stepped crater floor with steep lip. | Pit diameter rarely exceeds 3 mm; check if light catches the bottom surface. |
| **Rust vs. Corrosion** | Rust is ferric oxide (orange/brown powdery surface layer); corrosion encompasses substrate degradation, pitting, thinning, and non-ferrous oxides. | If surface can be wiped and base metal is intact = Rust. If metal is etched, scaled, or pitted = Corrosion. |
| **Excess Weld vs. Spatter vs. Porosity** | Excess weld = continuous bead overfill; Spatter = isolated spherical droplets; Porosity = dark gas entrapment pinholes in bead. | Spatter is convex droplet; porosity is concave pinhole. |
| **Defect vs. Artifact (Glare/Shadow/Blur)** | Glare moves when videoscope probe articulates; real defects maintain fixed spatial coordinate on asset surface. | If feature disappears or shifts with illumination angle = Optical Artifact. |

## 5. Decision Tree for Visual Classification

```
1. Is the feature on or immediately adjacent to a weld seam?
   ├── Yes:
   │   ├── Convex continuous bead overfill? ──────────────> Class 10 (Excess Weld Metal)
   │   ├── Small convex spherical droplets? ──────────────> Class 11 (Weld Spatter)
   │   ├── Concave dark round gas pinhole? ───────────────> Class 12 (Weld Porosity)
   │   └── Sharp linear fissure in weld/HAZ? ─────────────> Class 1 (Crack)
   └── No:
2. Is the feature predominantly linear (length >> width)?
   ├── Jagged, tortuous path, dark trough, sharp tips? ───> Class 1 (Crack)
   ├── Straight/arc, constant width, reflective ridges? ──> Class 5 (Scratch)
   └── Polygonal interconnected mesh on hot component? ───> Class 13 (Thermal Cracking)
3. Is the feature a depression or void (2D / 3D aspect)?
   ├── Engineered circular opening / through breach? ─────> Class 2 (Hole)
   ├── Localized cluster of rough micro-craters? ─────────> Class 3 (Pitting)
   ├── Smooth concave depression without surface tear? ───> Class 8 (Dent)
   ├── Flaked-off layer with crisp fracture step? ────────> Class 9 (Chipping / Spalling)
   └── Large non-through casting / erosion pocket? ───────> Class 4 (Cavity / Void)
4. Is the feature a surface accumulation or color alteration?
   ├── Orange / amber / reddish-brown ferrous film? ──────> Class 6 (Rust)
   ├── Diffuse substrate attack, scaling, etching? ───────> Class 7 (Corrosion)
   └── External particulate, soot, grease, deposit? ──────> Class 14 (Dirt / Deposit)
5. Cannot be confirmed due to glare, blur, or ambiguity? ──> Class 15 (Unknown / Ambiguous)
```

## 6. Training Class Mapping Recommendation

For initial object detector model training (e.g. YOLOv8 / YOLOv11 edge model), the 15 taxonomy classes can be mapped into target detector heads based on inspection priority and dataset availability.

| Detector Model Class ID | Taxonomy Class Source | Rationale |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| `0` | **Crack** (Class 1) | Highest critical failure mode (Immediate Flight/Operation Grounding) |
| `1` | **Hole** (Class 2) | Critical geometry distinction (Prevent hole-vs-crack confusion) |
| `2` | **Pitting** (Class 3) | Localized cavitation / fatigue initiation site |
| `3` | **Scratch** (Class 5) | Superficial mechanical groove (High volume false-positive reducer) |
| `4` | **Corrosion_Rust** (Classes 6 & 7) | Combined chemical surface degradation category |
| `5` | **Dent** (Class 8) | Mechanical impact deformation |
| `6` | **Chipping_Spalling** (Class 9) | Coating loss / edge fracture |
| `7` | **Weld_Defect** (Classes 10, 11, 12) | Grouped welding quality anomalies |
| `8` | **Thermal_Cracking** (Class 13) | Thermal fatigue crazing network |
| `9` | **Deposit_Fouling** (Class 14) | Non-destructive accumulation |

*Note*: Class 15 (Unknown) is never mapped to a trained positive class; it serves as an inspector triaging flag and triggers recommendations for physical re-inspection.
